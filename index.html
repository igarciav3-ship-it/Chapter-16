Grammar Focus - What is Parallel Structure?

"One use of a conjunction is to connect words or phrases that have the SAME grammatical function." 🤝
Conjunctions used: AND, BUT, OR, NOR (COORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS)
Example Structure What's Connected
(a) Steve and his friend are coming. noun + and + noun Two nouns
(b) Susan raised her hand and snapped her fingers. verb + and + verb Two verbs
(c) He is waving his arms and (is) shouting. verb + and + verb Two verbs
(d) These shoes are old but comfortable. adjective + but + adjective Two adjectives
(e) He wants to watch TV or (to) listen to music. infinitive + or + infinitive Two infinitives

Important Notes on Omission:

1. Auxiliary Verbs

The second auxiliary may be omitted if it's the same as the first:
He is waving and (is) shouting.

2. Infinitives

The second "to" is usually omitted:
He wants to watch TV or (to) listen.

Grammar Focus - Commas in Parallel Structure

1

No Commas with TWO Items

(a) Steve and Joe are in class. ✅ NO comma
(b) Steve , and Joe are in class. ❌ INCORRECT

"Two items? No comma!" 🚫

2

Commas with THREE or More Items

(c) Steve , Joe and Rita are in class.
(d) Steve , Joe , and Rita are in class.
(e) Steve , Joe , Rita , Jan and Kim...

"The comma before 'and' (Oxford comma) is OPTIONAL!" 🎤

Punctuation Tip

Punctuation brings clarity to your academic writing. When listing three or more items in an essay, using clear commas ensures your reader follows your ideas effortlessly!

Connecting Independent Clauses with AND & BUT

An independent clause = a complete sentence (Subject + Verb + Complete Thought).
It was raining hard. (S+V) There was a strong wind. (S+V)

3 Correct Ways to Connect Independent Clauses:

Method 1 Use a PERIOD (.)

It was raining hard. There was a strong wind.

Method 2 Use a SEMICOLON (;)

It was raining hard; there was a strong wind.

Method 3 COMMA + AND / BUT

It was raining hard, and there was a strong wind.

NEVER use just a COMMA (Comma Splice)!

❌ It was raining hard, there was a strong wind. (INCORRECT - A comma alone is NOT enough!)

Paired Conjunctions (Correlative Conjunctions)

Paired conjunctions come in pairs to link two parallel words or phrases. Whatever structure follows the first word must match the structure following the second word.

1. Both ... and

Connects two positive ideas. Always takes a plural verb when connecting two subjects!

• Both my sister and my brother are coming.

2. Not only ... but also

Emphasizes both elements. The verb agrees with the subject closest to it.

• Not only the teacher but also the students were late.

3. Either ... or

Indicates a choice between two options. Verb agrees with the closest subject.

• Either Maria or her sisters have the keys.

4. Neither ... nor

Negates both options (not one, and not the other). Verb agrees with the closest subject.

• Neither the food nor the drinks were ready.
Parallel Form Rule with Paired Conjunctions:

Make sure the grammatical components matching the pair are structurally identical:

  • ✅ They noted not only the price [noun] but also the quality [noun].
  • ❌ They noted not only the price [noun] but also were checking quality [verb phrase].
